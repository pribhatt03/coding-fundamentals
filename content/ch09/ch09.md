# 9. Adding and transforming columns

So far you've taken things out of a data frame. Now you'll put new things
in. Store something under a column name that doesn't exist yet, and R
creates that column. It's the same `<-` you've used since module 3, pointed
at a column.

That's useful when you've collected new data, or when you want a new
variable worked out from ones you already have — like each patient's mean
arterial pressure, from their two blood pressures.

## Changing a column you already have

Storing into a column that already exists replaces it, silently. That's
often exactly what you want, but it's worth being deliberate about, because
nothing stops you writing over a column you still needed.
